I.My story

From the code to the rack it runs on.

I like understanding the whole system: the screen a user clicks on, the code and database behind it, and the server rack it all runs on. That’s what drew me to a BSc in Computing with IT Management at TU Dublin, where I studied software development, systems administration, networking, IT infrastructure and databases.

During my software engineering internship at SAP, I co-developed Unified Calendar, a team scheduling web app. I led a weekend debugging effort that fixed a critical bug where the app’s interface and database fell out of sync, and I looked after the physical servers our development environments ran on. Before that, I spent over two years in technical support at MoneyGram. I’m CompTIA A+ certified, and I’m working towards CompTIA Security+ and Microsoft AZ-900 and SC-900.

Project · 2026 In progress

FishScan.

A Yuka-style scanner for aquarium fish: point your phone at a fish to find out its species and check it for visible signs of disease.

  • On the phone, a detector finds the fish and a classifier names the species, filtered by the tank’s water type
  • A FastAPI service then checks the fish for visible signs such as white spots or frayed fins
  • A rule-based layer and a PostgreSQL knowledge base match those signs to diseases that species can actually get in that water
  • 24 automated Python tests pass; the Flutter app and the models are still being built and trained
  • Flutter
  • Dart
  • Python
  • FastAPI
  • PostgreSQL
  • Machine learning
  1. Stage 1 · on the phone
  2. Find the fishAn object detector locates the fish in the camera view.
  3. Name the speciesA classifier, limited to fish that live in your tank’s water.
  4. Stage 2 · in the cloud
  5. Spot the signsA second model looks for white spots, frayed fins and more.
  6. Match likely diseasesRules and a PostgreSQL knowledge base keep only diseases that species can get.
